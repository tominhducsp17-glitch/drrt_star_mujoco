# Benchmarks, alternative contribution angles ("Angle D"), and industry relevance for a 4-arm shared-workspace sorting cell

Context for the reader: the system under discussion has 4 UR10e + Robotiq 2F-85 in MuJoCo around a 1.3 m table, dRRT* with event-driven asynchronous replanning, online greedy allocation with an elliptic hitbox penalty (taken from Oberthür et al., IFAC 2025), a CP-SAT offline scheduling reference, overhead RGB-D colour perception, and scripted grasping. It measured +30% throughput vs a turn-based baseline and 0 collisions on 400 picks. These figures come from the project itself, not from external sources.

Research date: 2026-11-16. About 20 tool calls. Several sources were read from the primary PDF/HTML; vendor figures are marked as vendor claims.

## 1. Existing benchmarks, simulators and evaluation practice for multi-arm (3+) shared-workspace manipulation

### Takeaway
No widely adopted open benchmark exists for **multi-arm (3+) shared-table pick-and-place/sorting with throughput as the metric**. What exists falls into three groups: (a) multi-arm *motion-planning* benchmarks (CMU xECBS scenes, the IROS 2025 MR-shortcut dataset, the RoboBallet workcells), which report success rate, planning time and path cost/execution time; (b) learning benchmarks for multi-agent manipulation (RoboFactory/ManiSkill, ICCV 2025), which report task success; and (c) scheduling papers (El Ghazi 2024 / Oberthür 2025, SCoBA), which report makespan or the fraction of tasks lost on small instance sets of about 10 instances per configuration, or 100 trials. Reviewers in this area typically expect success rate, planning time with a timeout, makespan/execution time, and collision count, measured over tens to hundreds of instances with means and error bars.

### Cited Findings

**xCBS / xECBS: "Accelerating Search-Based Planning for Multi-Robot Manipulation by Leveraging Online-Generated Experiences"** (Shaoul, Mishani, Likhachev, Li; CMU; arXiv 2404.00143, Mar 2024; venue not stated on the arXiv page) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00143)
- Summary: The paper proposes experience-reusing variants of CBS/ECBS for multi-arm manipulation planning. It evaluates them on scalability scenes with 2–10 Franka arms and on two task-like scenes: **8-arm shelf rearrangement and 4-arm bin-picking**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00143)
- Metrics: success rate, planning time, and solution cost (total joint motion in rad); the scalability study also reports collision checks. Baselines: PRM, RRT-Connect (OMPL), **dRRT\***, PP, CBS, ECBS, and CBS-MP. There are 50 start/goal problems per scene, a **60 s timeout** after which a run counts as a failure, and 7 scenes in total (the page text says "77", which looks like a typo). The page does not say whether the benchmark is released. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00143)

**Generalized ECBS / M-RAMP: "Unconstraining Multi-Robot Manipulation: Enabling Arbitrary Constraints in ECBS with Bounded Sub-Optimality"** (arXiv 2405.01772, May 2024) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01772)
- Summary (from search snippet only, full text not read): the paper evaluates teams of high-DoF manipulators in a shared workspace with 4, 6 and 8 arms. It argues that Generalized ECBS beats complete methods (ECBS) and incomplete methods (Prioritized Planning). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01772)

**"Benchmarking Shortcutting Techniques for Multi-Robot-Arm Motion Planning"** (Philip Huang, Yorai Shaoul, Jiaoyang Li; CMU; IROS 2025; arXiv 2508.05027) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05027v1), [project page](https://philip-huang.github.io/mr-shortcut)
- Summary (from search snippets): the paper quantitatively compares shortcutting methods on simulated multi-arm scenarios using 1034 multi-arm trajectories generated by two planners. It finds that shortcutting one arm can create collisions with another, so multi-robot-aware shortcutting is needed. The authors released the **dataset and code**. — [project page](https://philip-huang.github.io/mr-shortcut)
- This is the closest thing to an open multi-arm motion benchmark with released data, but it covers trajectory post-processing, not task-level throughput.

**RoboBallet: "Planning for Multi-Robot Reaching with Graph Neural Networks and Reinforcement Learning"** (Lai, Go, Kröger, Schaal, Li, Allen, Scholz; Google DeepMind / Intrinsic / UCL; **Science Robotics, Sept 2025**; arXiv 2509.05397) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05397)
- Summary: a GNN policy trained with TD3+HER jointly does task allocation, scheduling and motion planning for **up to 8 Franka arms** (56-D) and up to 40 reaching tasks among about 30 random obstacles. A 4-arm model was validated on a real workcell. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05397)
- Metrics: trajectory execution time (primary), task solve rate, collision count, and inference time per step. Baselines: RRT-Connect + shortcutting with exhaustive scheduling/IK sampling (10 sets of 20 tasks, 4 robots, pre-assigned tasks) and a random-schedule baseline. Evaluation used 10 task sets, 10 placement configurations for scaling, and 10 task sets for layout optimisation; random seeds are not reported. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05397)

**RoboFactory: "Exploring Embodied Agent Collaboration with Compositional Constraints"** (Qin et al.; **ICCV 2025**; arXiv 2503.16408; code at GitHub MARS-EAI/RoboFactory) — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Qin_RoboFactory_Exploring_Embodied_Agent_Collaboration_with_Compositional_Constraints_ICCV_2025_paper.html), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.16408)
- Summary: a multi-agent embodied manipulation benchmark built on **ManiSkill**, with 11 tasks using varying numbers of Franka arms. It generates collaborative demonstration data under "compositional constraints" (logical, spatial, temporal) and offers 150 pre-collected demos per task and four multi-agent imitation-learning baselines. — [search summary of arXiv/CVF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.16408)
- Relevance: open and multi-arm, but aimed at imitation learning and VLA research, with success rate as the metric. It is not a throughput or scheduling benchmark.

**DG-MAP: "Diffusion-Guided Multi-Arm Motion Planning"** (arXiv 2509.08160, 2025) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08160)
- Summary (search snippet): a four-arm UR5 setup around a central bin picking YCB objects. It reports 89% success over 100 trials, with success defined as no inter-arm or ground collisions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08160). Note: this is the closest *hardware/geometry* analogue to a 4-arm UR cell around a table.

**Multi-arm handoff TAMP: "Anytime Multi-arm Task and Motion Planning for Pick-and-Place of Individual Objects via Handoffs"** (arXiv 1905.03179, 2019) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.03179)
- Summary (search snippet): a planner for chains of 2–5 KUKA iiwa arms that pass objects to each other, including a benchmark variant with obstacles. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.03179)

**El Ghazi et al. (2024) problem plus Oberthür et al., "Scheduling of Pick-and-Place Tasks in Shared Workspaces Using a Trajectory-Free Approximation Method"** (IFAC PapersOnLine 59-10, 2025, pp. 1384–1389, DOI 10.1016/j.ifacol.2025.09.233, open access) — [UMONS PDF](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/55073/1/Version%20publi%c3%a9e.pdf)
- Summary: the paper addresses **four heterogeneous robot arms in a limited shared workspace**, a problem introduced by El Ghazi et al. (2024), whose MILP uses cube-discretised trajectory simulations and gets low makespan at high compute cost. Oberthür et al. replace that with a trajectory-free MILP that uses only trajectory durations and positions, plus **parabolic or elliptic "hitboxes"** to predict inter-robot collisions. — [PDF](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/55073/1/Version%20publi%c3%a9e.pdf)
- Experimental protocol: n = 4 robots; m = 4, 6, 8 or 10 objects; workspace side 0.35 ("tight") or 0.55 ("sparse"); **10 instances per (m, Δ)**; Gurobi 10 with a 1 h limit. The El Ghazi model uses q = 6 cubes per dimension and 100 ms slots. Paths and durations are computed with ROS. — [PDF](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/55073/1/Version%20publi%c3%a9e.pdf)
- Hitbox fitting: the authors scored the hitbox's collision predictions against El Ghazi's simulation with F1. The best ellipse (d\*E = 0.162) scored F1 = 0.931 (precision 0.914, recall 0.949) and the best parabola (d\*P = 0.232) scored F1 = 0.926. They chose the ellipse because it misses fewer collisions. The model builds and solves in ≤ 0.4 s. It is worse than El Ghazi for ≤ 8 objects but better for 10 objects, where El Ghazi's 1 h Gurobi gap grows. — [PDF](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/55073/1/Version%20publi%c3%a9e.pdf)
- **Stated limitation (important for novelty):** "our hitbox does not detect all collisions…" The schedules "could be tested with El Ghazi's simulations to check the percentage of" collisions. In other words, they did **not execute** hitbox schedules with a real motion planner or in physics simulation. — [PDF](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/55073/1/Version%20publi%c3%a9e.pdf)

**ROS-Gazebo MILP + heuristic coordination: "High-level multi-robot coordination in a shared workspace for pick-and-place operations using MILP optimization and heuristic for conflict resolution"** (Robotics and Autonomous Systems, 2026, ScienceDirect S0921889026001387) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921889026001387)
- Summary (search snippet only; full text not read, so authors are unverified): a high-level coordinator for robots with overlapping targets, validated in ROS-Gazebo on **240 instances**. The exact MILP proves optimality for 55% of 4-object cases and none at ≥ 6 objects, so the paper relies on a heuristic. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921889026001387). *Uncertain:* it may be the journal follow-up of El Ghazi et al.; check.

**SCoBA: "Dynamic Multi-Robot Task Allocation under Uncertainty and Temporal Constraints"** (Choudhury, Gupta, Kochenderfer, Sadigh, Bohg; Stanford; RSS 2020 per the reference list / NSF PAR copy — venue *uncertain*, verify) — [NSF PAR PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10208439)
- Summary: hierarchical allocation for tasks arriving online, with time windows and stochastic success. One domain has **3 arms along a moving conveyor** with mutually exclusive adjacent workspaces, picking objects into bins, where objects missed by all arms must be hand-sorted. Baselines include EDD, Hungarian, and MCTS-type methods; the metric is the proportion of objects lost per trial, **averaged over 100 trials, T = 500 steps**, with standard-error bars. — [NSF PAR PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10208439)

**Multi-agent DRL in Isaac Sim** (PMC12655905, 2025) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12655905/)
- Summary (search snippet): manipulators with overlapping workspaces doing cube-to-box transfer in Isaac Sim. It reports 92% success and more than 50% task-time reduction vs a single arm. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12655905/)

### Inferences
- The **de facto metric set** a reviewer will look for in this niche is: success rate; planning time with a fixed timeout (60 s in xECBS); makespan or execution time (RoboBallet, Oberthür); collision count/rate (RoboBallet, DG-MAP); and, for online or dynamic settings, the fraction of tasks lost or missed (SCoBA). For a throughput paper, picks/min or makespan per N objects are the natural fit.
- The **typical sample sizes** are about 10 instances per configuration (Oberthür, RoboBallet), 50 problems per scene (xECBS), 100 trials (SCoBA, DG-MAP), and 240 instances (RAS 2026). Against these, the project's "400 picks" is credible in count. It should still be reported as N scenes × M seeds with mean ± std or 95% CI, plus a paired comparison against the turn-based baseline on the same scenes.
- Expected baselines for this system: turn-based or mutex (already done); prioritized planning; dRRT\* without async replanning; the Oberthür hitbox MILP (or the CP-SAT reference) as an offline bound; and possibly xECBS or another CBS-style planner (xECBS reports dRRT\* as a baseline, so reviewers know it).
- The gap that stands out: **no open benchmark combines (i) 3+ arms on one shared table, (ii) perception-driven pick/sort tasks, and (iii) throughput or makespan as the metric with a physics simulator.** RoboFactory is open but targets learning and success rate; xECBS/MR-shortcut are motion-only; Oberthür/El Ghazi are scheduling-only without execution.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether the xECBS scenes (8-arm shelf, 4-arm bin picking) are released; the arXiv HTML does not say.
- I did not find BEHAVIOR/RoboCasa-style multi-robot *manipulation* settings with 3+ arms beyond RoboFactory. No "multi-arm bin-picking dataset" with 3+ arms turned up.
- Full texts of the RAS 2026 MILP paper and the 2405.01772 paper were not read; their details rest on search snippets.
- The SCoBA venue was not confirmed from the paper itself.

## 2. Candidate "Angle D" contributions and their novelty

### Takeaway
The strongest open angle is **closing the loop on Oberthür/El Ghazi**: their hitbox scheduler was never executed with a real motion planner, while this system executes it with dRRT\* and measures collisions and throughput. Turning that into an **open 4-arm sorting-cell benchmark plus an arm-count/table-size interference scaling study** is only partially covered (RoboBallet does scaling for *reaching*, not shared-table pick-and-place). Layout optimisation for throughput is partially done (RoboBallet, single-robot base placement work). Dynamic conveyor arrivals are covered in allocation-only form (SCoBA) and in industry products, but not with a motion-planned shared workspace. Human-in-the-loop was not researched in depth.

### Cited Findings and novelty judgments

**D1. Open benchmark/release for multi-arm sorting cells.** Novelty: **open, partial overlap.**
- Closest: RoboFactory (ICCV 2025) is open and multi-arm on ManiSkill but focuses on imitation learning and success rate — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Qin_RoboFactory_Exploring_Embodied_Agent_Collaboration_with_Compositional_Constraints_ICCV_2025_paper.html). MR-shortcut released a multi-arm trajectory dataset and code for path post-processing only — [project](https://philip-huang.github.io/mr-shortcut). The El Ghazi/Oberthür instances are a 4-arm shared-workspace pick-and-place *scheduling* problem whose F1 evaluation uses El Ghazi's instance set — [PDF](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/55073/1/Version%20publi%c3%a9e.pdf). I found no public release of those instances.
- Evidence of the gap: Oberthür et al. state the hitbox misses some collisions and that schedules should be checked against simulation — [PDF](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/55073/1/Version%20publi%c3%a9e.pdf).

**D2. "How many arms should share a table?" Throughput vs number of arms and interference.** Novelty: **partial.**
- RoboBallet reports average execution time falling from about 7.5 s with 4 robots to 4.5 s with 8 robots, over 10 placement configurations — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05397). The paper states this is a 60% reduction, but by arithmetic it is 40%, so this looks like an error in the paper. That result covers reaching tasks among obstacles, not shared-table pick-and-place with drop-off trays.
- xECBS scales *planning* to 2–10 arms (success and planning time), not task throughput — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00143).
- The Isaac Sim DRL paper reports more than 50% task-time reduction vs a single arm — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12655905/). This is a single comparison point, not a scaling curve.
- Oberthür varies object count (4–10) and workspace tightness (0.35 vs 0.55) with 4 robots fixed, so it does not vary the number of arms — [PDF](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/55073/1/Version%20publi%c3%a9e.pdf).

**D3. Layout optimisation (robot base plus tray placement) for throughput.** Novelty: **partial; done for single robots and for multi-arm reaching.**
- RoboBallet optimised the placement of 4–8 robots with Google Vizier (GP bandit) and improved execution time "by up to 33%" over default corner placements, mostly through more even task distribution — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05397).
- Single-robot base placement for pick-and-place sequences (IFAC 2024, ScienceDirect S2405896324014782) reports up to 44% cycle-time and 53% energy reduction, best vs worst, using a trajectory planner inside the optimiser — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405896324014782) (from search summary; full text not read).
- TU Wien ACIN optimises TCP plus base placement jointly and reports 41% cycle-time reduction vs prior methods on a KUKA iiwa — [ACIN](https://www.acin.tuwien.ac.at/en/3tcp/) (search summary).
- Open sub-gap: jointly optimising **tray/drop-off placement and arm bases for a multi-arm shared-table sorting cell, scored by executed throughput with a multi-arm planner**. I found no paper doing exactly this, but the search was limited.

**D4. Dynamic arrivals (conveyor) with multi-arm picking scheduling.** Novelty: **partial. Allocation-level work exists, and industry products solve load balancing. The open part is combining dynamic arrivals with an overlapping, motion-planned shared workspace.**
- SCoBA: 3 arms along a conveyor with **mutually exclusive adjacent workspaces**, so there is no shared-zone collision reasoning, and it is evaluated on lost-object proportion — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10208439).
- An older game-theoretic paper on multi-robot conveyor coordination (RCIM, pre-2019) and two-robot conveyor sequencing papers exist — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257242303_Multirobot_coordination_in_pick-and-place_tasks_on_a_moving_conveyor), [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359805497_Optimal_Strategy_for_Pick-and-Place_System_with_two_Robots) (not read in full).
- An MDPI *Robotics* paper (vol. 14, issue 11; year assumed 2025, unverified) formulates line balancing of robots on a common flow as an MDP with rollout — [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/2218-6581/14/11/150) (search snippet only; fetch blocked).
- Industry already has load balancing and bypass for conveyor robots (FANUC iRPickTool, ABB PickMaster; see section 3). An academic contribution would therefore need to address *overlapping* work areas, which these products avoid by giving each robot its own conveyor work area.

**D5. Executing hitbox-based allocation with a real multi-arm planner (validation of Oberthür).** Novelty: **open, and the closest fit to the existing system.**
- Oberthür et al. never execute schedules. Their own collision ground truth is a cube-discretised approximation, and they name validation as future work — [PDF](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/55073/1/Version%20publi%c3%a9e.pdf).
- The project's measured result (0 collisions over 400 picks with dRRT\* plus async replanning, +30% vs turn-based) directly answers "how safe and how fast are hitbox schedules when executed?". This is project data, not externally sourced.

**D6. Human-in-the-loop.** Novelty: **not assessed.** I found no specific sources within the tool budget.

### Inferences
- Ranked by fit and novelty: (1) **D5 + D1**: release the 4-arm MuJoCo sorting cell as a benchmark with executed metrics, using Oberthür/El Ghazi-style instance families (m objects × tight/sparse) so results are directly comparable. (2) **D2**: sweep 2/3/4(/6) arms × table size × object density, with throughput and wait/replan time as outcomes; RoboBallet's reaching-only scaling is the reference to differentiate from. (3) **D3**: tray and base placement optimisation; RoboBallet already shows up to 33% from placement, so novelty must come from pick-and-place with trays plus executed planning. (4) **D4**: conveyor arrivals with overlapping zones, positioned against SCoBA's exclusive workspaces and vendor load balancing.
- To compare fairly with Oberthür's protocol, report results by (number of objects, workspace tightness) with 10 or more instances each, alongside makespan RPD vs the CP-SAT reference.

### Gaps
- I found no paper on "how many arms should share a table" for pick-and-place specifically; absence of evidence within about 20 searches, not proof.
- Human-in-the-loop multi-arm sorting was not researched.
- The El Ghazi et al. (2024) original paper (venue, availability of instances) was not located directly.

## 3. Industry relevance of multi-robot picking and sorting cells

### Takeaway
Multi-robot conveyor picking is a mature, productised capability. FANUC iRPickTool and ABB PickMaster provide multi-robot **line tracking with load balancing** (equal or biased splits, redistribution when a robot stops, bypassing so upstream robots don't consume everything). Recycling (AMP Robotics) and parcel induction (Ambi, Plus One, Dexterity) ship two-arm or multi-arm cells sold on picks-per-minute or parcels-per-hour. Industry cares about throughput, load balance, uptime when a robot stops, and simulation before deployment. A portfolio project maps onto this when it shows scheduling or load balancing, collision-free shared zones, perception-driven picking, and ideally ROS 2 / MoveIt 2 integration (the last point is my inference; I did not collect job-description evidence).

### Inferences
- **What industry optimises:** picks/min (throughput) and outfeed consistency; load share across robots, including biased splits; graceful degradation when one robot stops; and miss handling (bypass, ATC, downstream catch). It also optimises offline simulation before commissioning (iRPickPRO, PickMaster Twin). These map directly onto the project's throughput metric, its online allocation, and a possible "robot dropout" experiment.
- **Where the project differs from industry practice:** vendor products give each robot its own conveyor work area along the belt and coordinate mostly by item assignment.
